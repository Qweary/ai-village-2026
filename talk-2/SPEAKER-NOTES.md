`SCREEN: slide 1.` Up before you speak. **No title slide.** Two quoted sentences from a published incident report, with the source line naming the bug tracker report and the bug number.

### SAY AS WRITTEN

> "Read that while I talk. Those are two sentences from a certificate authority's incident report, about their own software, written by them. >

### Three attribution rules that are load-bearing

1. **Measure the live window, not the code's age.** The correct answer is **190 days [N]**, from the flag being enabled. The other two candidate dates give **311 [N]** and **219 [N]**. Say the right one **and say why it is the right one.** Somebody in that room subtracts dates for a living.
2. **The interval is your arithmetic, not their wording.** They publish dates and no duration. Give the dates, do the subtraction out loud, and the claim becomes unattackable. **Never attribute a duration to them.**
3. **The certificate figures are from the forum post, not the bug.** Say "their forum post" when you say them. The percentage is their wording and their denominator, which is why it is safe to quote. You are not dividing anything.

**Say the credit and mean it.** They found it, they published it, and they published the reason both their tests missed it. That is the only reason any of us can learn from it. **A room that hears you being smug about somebody else's incident report will not believe your own confession twenty seconds later.**

# MOVE 2. 2:30 to 5:15. The frame, paid immediately

`SCREEN: slide 2.` Three measurements, three separate populations, n printed on the surface.

`DO:` This is the only section of the hour where the talk is not attackable, and it funds everything after it. **Spend the numbers before you spend any argument.**

**The frame, in one breath.** Everything I build now compiles differently every time I run it. Not metaphorically. The same input produces a different artifact, the difference is measurable, and I have measured it three times with three different instruments.

### The three measurements

**One. Scoring, blind.** The same byte-identical text, under the same procedure, came back with **two different scores.** Not similar text. Not a regeneration. The same bytes.
**No figure here and no n. Observation only, no size and no direction.** If move 11 gets cut, say the one-line version here: *"I had two numbers in this slot and I pulled them, because when I went back for the transcripts they were gone."*

**Two. Scoring, unblinded, on a fixed input.** I ran the unblinded scorer on identical input and **I am not going to give you the scores**, for the same reason I did not give you the two a moment ago. What is underneath them needs no number and you can read it in the source in a minute: **one model call writes the revised transcript, scores that transcript, and issues the verdict on itself.** A loop built that way cannot tell you whether the rewrite helped, because the grader and the thing being graded are one call.
**No figure here, no n, and no count of runs. Do not restore the values in digits or in paraphrase.**

**Three. The thing that generates, not the thing that scores.** The same three-word brief, run twice, took **321 seconds and 660 seconds [N]**, and produced **4,764 words and 10,660 words [N]**. **[n = 2 runs. Say that. It is a pair, not a distribution, and somebody will ask.]**

### The landing

Three instruments, three separate populations, all pointing the same way, and **only the third still carries a figure.**

**Do not add them together and do not quote a combined figure.** They are not one experiment. That comes back in move 11.

Then the consequence for engineering practice, which is why this is a talk and not a slide: **you do not have a fixed artifact to reason about. You have a distribution.** If you have never run the same thing several times and looked at the spread, you do not yet know what any of your numbers mean. It takes an afternoon.

# MOVE 3. 5:15 to 8:30. The catalogue, and now it is mine

`SCREEN: slide 3.` The shape of a sweep whose exit status is reset before anything reads it. The slide names where this lives: the checking in an AI system is ordinary code wrapped around the model.

`DO:` Turn the talk inward in one line. That was somebody else's, and it is easy to be generous about somebody else's. **Here are five of mine.**

### The overnight sentence. About eight seconds. Then go.

**SAY AS WRITTEN**

> "Some of you watched a machine last night whose runs kept coming back improved. This morning I am going to tell you why I stopped believing that number. If you were not there, you have missed nothing."

**Then straight into the five instruments.** Do not expand it, do not apologise, and do not relitigate the demo. **Lead with why you stopped believing the number, never with the playback.** If somebody asks specifically about the recorded mode, that is a question, and the answer is prepared at exposure 13.

### One. The verifier, with the mechanism

`DO:` I had a component whose whole job was to look at a change and report whether it made things better or worse. **Inside its own instructions, before it evaluated anything, was a line naming the range of improvement it should report.**

So it reported improvement.

**SAY AS WRITTEN**

> "Running that system could never have caught it. Running it produced exactly the output it was instructed to produce. Only reading the source against the claim did."

**Now the thirty seconds that earn their place.** *(This is the aside you drop if the room is heavily returning. Default is to tell it.)*

When I went looking at what the harness did with that component's output, I found it had never read it.

- **The verdict reader matched zero of thirteen shipped results. [N]** Every published verdict was a hardcoded default string.
- **The score reader matched two of thirteen. [N]** The other eleven were arithmetic: take the number you started with and add two.
- **All thirteen published results are exactly plus two point zero. [N]** Two generations of the harness, four modes, three rounds, and the set of distinct published deltas has one element in it.
- **The prompt asked for plus one to plus three. The fallback added exactly two, the midpoint. [N]** The steering sentence and the arithmetic default agreed with each other.

**Say the shape clearly, because it is different from the cold open.** Those two had nothing in common. One was a sentence in a prompt. The other was an error-path default in different code written months apart. They were not two checks sharing an assumption. **They were two unrelated defects that happened to produce identical output, and the artifact is where they agreed, so the artifact could not tell you either one existed.**

**One claim you must not make here.** Do not say the two captures where the reader worked were fabricated. They were genuine reads. The defensible claim is about the artifact and it is stronger: **a published result carried no information that could distinguish a measured number from a manufactured one.** That holds for all thirteen, and it survives somebody reading the source.

### Two, three, four. One line each, no forensics

- A gate that was green over the exact file it was written to guard.
- A sweep that checked a list of files, where a command substitution in the middle of it reset the exit status before anything read it. **That check could not fail. Not would not. Could not.**
- A list of patterns that contained the patterns' own words, which turned the check into a silent no-op.

### Five. At one remove

When I repaired one of those gates, it hard-failed on real text while **its own seventy-one fixtures stayed green. [N]** The fixtures and the repair were written by the same person from the same idea about where the problem was, **so the fixtures could only ever confirm the aim I already had.**

**One sentence of callback, then move.** That is the same shape as the two tests in the opening story. One assumption, two things agreeing, and the agreement is what made it invisible. **It is not a rare shape. It is the default shape.**

### SAY AS WRITTEN. The landing, and it is the most disarming thing the talk has

**Do not soften this and do not follow it with a recommendation.**

> "Now the part I want to be very clear about, because it is the part a talk like this normally gets wrong.
>
> Not one of those was caught by a control. Not one. Every single one was caught by a person opening a file and reading it against what it claimed.
>
> And the one gate I had that could see an entire class of these was, on the worst instance in the set, blind by construction. I will show you that one in half an hour.
>
> So I am not going to stand here and tell you that a better scanner would have saved me. I had scanners. They were green. Some of them were green because they were structurally incapable of being anything else."

**Do not name the reason here.** That landing is the first proof of the argument move 6 states out loud, and the room does not have the vocabulary yet. **Let them feel the proof now and be given the name for it later.** A forward reference spends the hinge's best moment eight minutes early.

# MOVE 4. 8:30 to 9:30. The handoff

`SCREEN: slide 4.` One control, the four rules, and the line to write the name on.

One minute, and every second of it is doing work.

`DO:` **Lean on what just happened.** I have just told you five of mine failed. In about five minutes I am going to run one of my own through this thing and fail it in front of you, and it is the one I rely on most.

**Then hand the room the subject matter.** Pick one control. Not the one you should pick. **The one you actually trust.** If somebody asked you what stops the bad thing, the one you would name.

Write its name down where nobody can see it. **Nothing is collected, nothing goes on a screen, and I do not want to know what you wrote.**

### The three rules. Say all three at setup

Every one of them was added because running this on real systems watched it go wrong.

1. **One control, not a pipeline.** If what you wrote has the word "and" in it, you have named two things and you are about to answer five questions about whichever one is convenient. **Keep the one that does the stopping.** This is the cheapest way in the room to award yourself a good verdict honestly.
2. **Answers are nouns, actions, months, and numbers.** If you catch yourself writing a sentence with the word "because" in it, you are arguing with the card, and the card does not care.
   **Say rule 2 hard.** It is the enforcement mechanism for the entire instrument. Every defence of a weak control needs a subordinate clause and there is nowhere to put one.
   **Say the enumeration exactly as written.** Those eight words are printed on the slide in front of them and on both faces of the card.
3. **This measures what you can retrieve, not what is true.** Two people in this room with the same control and different access will get different verdicts. **That is the instrument working, not failing.** A control whose firing leaves no trace you can get at is, for every decision you are going to make about it, the same as one that does not fire.

**The fallback, in one line:** "No system in scope right now? Use the last one. Still nothing? Use the control you would tell a junior to trust."

**Do not preview the five questions.** Sequential reveal is the enforcement, and previewing destroys it.

Then: **"Hold your questions for nine minutes. I will tell you when."**

## 10b. The mechanism. About 40 seconds

`SCREEN: slide 13.` The split, drawn. N headers, N plus one pieces, the last piece running off the bottom edge.

`DO:` **Here is the shape, and I want you to check whether you own one.**

Records live in a text file, under repeated headers. To analyse them one at a time you split the file on the header pattern. Perfectly ordinary. **Most of you have written this.**

**A record's end is never written down anywhere.** Think about that for a second. It is defined only by where the next record starts. **The second form compounds it and it is the one that defeats the obvious fix.** If you split on one heading depth and the file also uses a shallower one, the shallower header is not a split point. > "a regex split leaves its final block unbounded, so the last record in a file silently absorbs everything after it"
